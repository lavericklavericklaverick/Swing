Model overview

This model is deliberately transparent and simple enough to inspect. It is not a substitute for a validated finite-element or multibody model.

  1. Geometry. Equal-height anchors are fixed. The highline attachment moves vertically and the jumper moves in the vertical plane.
  2. Highline elasticity. Each tension-only segment uses EA = F / epsilon and T = max(0, EA * (L / L0 - 1)). Rest lengths are inferred from pretension.
  3. Swing rope. The rope is a tension-only linear spring with phenomenological viscous damping.
  4. Backup line. Its linear mass is added to the effective highline mass, and its assumed damping ratio adds to the highline damping only. This approximates the loose taped line and dangling loops; it does not simulate their contact or changing slack geometry.
  5. Initial release. Static sag is solved with the jumper load present. At release, the jumper weight acts on the jumper rather than directly on the highline.
  6. Numerics. The coupled equations use fixed-step fourth-order Runge-Kutta integration. Repeat with half the timestep to check sensitivity.

Major assumptions and omissions

  • One static stretch datum is treated as linear material behavior.
  • Anchors are rigid, equal-height points and the attachment cannot slide.
  • Distributed webbing and backup-line dynamics are reduced to one-third of their combined mass as an effective moving mass in the lumped model.
  • The jumper is a point mass; knots, connectors, harness geometry, hysteresis, permanent set, drag, failure criteria, clearance, safety factors, anchor capacity, and redundancy are omitted.
  • Static manufacturer data may not represent dynamic loading. Damping is assumed rather than measured.

What is needed for a defensible model

Use complete loading and unloading force-extension curves at relevant loading rates; measured pretension and initial sag; compliant anchor and attachment behavior; effective lengths including knots and connectors; distributed line mass; measured damping/hysteresis; body geometry; uncertainty ranges; convergence studies; controlled validation tests; and independent review by suitably qualified people.
